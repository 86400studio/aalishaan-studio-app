Art Styles

How it was made changes how it feels. Find the marks that speak to you.

Gilded Peaks

Azurite and malachite — real mineral pigment — ridged over ink, with sky and water left as burnished gold leaf. The imperial blue-green landscape method.

See the artworks →

Rinpa Moon

Gold leaf laid edge to edge, silhouettes set flat against it, the moon simply left unpainted — the Rinpa screen method. Confidence measured in restraint.

See the artworks →

Four Gentlemen

Each blossom a few loaded touches, each cane one pull of the brush, no corrections possible. The Four Gentlemen tradition — calm earned through discipline.

See the artworks →

Scarlet Blossom

Freehand xieyi brushwork: loaded strokes placed once and left alone, ink trunk to scarlet cluster. Spontaneity that takes a lifetime to control.

See the artworks →

Moonlit Blossom

A ground mottled with blotted ink and salt until it reads like storm-stone; blossoms touched on in thick white gouache. Darkness built first, light placed last.

See the artworks →

Hand-Printed Ukiyo-e

Carved woodblocks, one per colour, pressed by hand with graded bokashi skies — the Edo printmaker's craft. Precision that still feels breathed, not produced.

See the artworks →

Mythic Voyage

Egg tempera over a gessoed board, gold leaf pressed along the crests — the icon-painter's discipline turned toward the sea. Flat, luminous, built stroke by stroke.

See the artworks →

Corsair Chronicles

Diluted sumi ink for the sea, vine charcoal for the ship — then spray and lightning lifted out with an eraser. Drawing by removal: the storm-maker's trick.

See the artworks →

Ghostly Armada

Ink rolled onto glass, the image wiped away, the plate pressed once — monotype, the unrepeatable print. What survives the wipe becomes the ghost.

See the artworks →

Marquetry Voyage

Every shape sawn from a different wood veneer, shaded in hot sand, inlaid flush — marquetry. No pigment anywhere; the grain paints the light.

See the artworks →

Neo-Classical Fantasy

A blackened board scratched with a stylus, the lines flooded with copper ink and gold — the scraperboard engraver's craft. It gleams like worked bronze.
