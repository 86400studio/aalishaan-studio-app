## What and why

The independent review of the S1.7 candidate at `0901d58` (round 2, 2026-11-26) returned two Blocking findings that lie inside `0006`'s functions — applied on TEST, so not editable — and that only a rule under the database's own row locks can close:

- **Finding 1 — a capture could clear a hold raised for a different payment.** When Razorpay's API listed as live a payment whose own signed `payment.failed` had already been processed, 0006's provider check left that row `failed` and could raise the clearable hold for it; the finalizer counts no failed row as live (`v_other_live`), so a timely signed capture of another payment cleared that hold, confirmed the order and queued `send_confirmation` (the 0006 record → Known limits (e)). Now a signed failure and a live observation of the same payment are a review no later capture clears, in either order of arrival (`payment_state_conflict`), and while a hold stands, a provider-check hold that names another payment counts as another live payment.
- **Finding 2 — a webhook that wins the order lock could erase evidence of an extra payment.** 0006's check wrote only while the order was pending and awaiting its payment, so a provider read that saw captured A and authorised B, finishing after A's webhook had confirmed the order, returned success with B and its money discarded (0006 contract 11; Known limits (b)). Now what the provider showed is kept for every linked attempt.

`86bb63c` (the owner, with another agent — 2026-11-26) wrote this file for both findings. The builder's review of `86bb63c` (2026-11-26, 23:05–23:51 UTC — the S1.7 record → H.16) found a remainder of finding 2 — the same discard when a signed event had just put a pending order under a review that is not a hold: a capture stored after the deadline, a money mismatch, an extra payment (that review's item R3-1, Blocking) — and two database comments that no longer said what the functions do (its R3-5). The file was amended before any apply (D-73 (b)): the provider check records every linked attempt's observation (*before the amendment:* only a captured order, or a pending order awaiting its payment under no review or under a hold), and both `COMMENT ON FUNCTION` texts and the file's header state 0007's rules. An independent check of the amended file (`4dead80`), still before any apply (2026-11-27 — safe to apply, with two fail-safe inconsistencies), found the provider check counting another live payment differently from the event path in two cases — a payment row without a payment id, and a standing hold that names another payment — each raising a hold the payment's own capture could not clear; `c642728` made the two functions count it the same way (contract 5). `payment_process_event`'s logic is `86bb63c`'s.

### Contracts (each has a test — "Verification")

`payment_process_event(uuid)` — 0006's body word for word except two hunks:

1. **A signed failure that contradicts a live API observation of the same payment is a review no capture clears.** In the failed branch, when the order carries a hold and an audit row `payment.hold.authorized_only` or `payment.hold.capture_unverified` with reason `provider_check` names this payment (`external_ref`), the outcome is `review:payment_state_conflict` — step (g): `needs_review = true`, `payment_hold = NULL`, one audit row `payment.review.payment_state_conflict` with the event's own money and ledger row. The payment's row moves to `failed` as before: the signed history is kept. Otherwise the failure is `recorded:failed`, as under 0006.
2. **While a hold stands, a provider-check hold naming another payment is another live payment.** Besides any other known payment of the order whose row has not failed (0006), `v_other_live` is true when the order carries a hold and a provider-check hold audit row names a payment other than this event's — a hold raised before 0007 can name a payment whose row already says `failed`. The capture of another payment is then `extra_payment`, never the capture that clears that hold.
3. **Everything else is 0006's** — the confirmation (the payment's row, the order's statuses and `confirmed_at`, its items' stage, one audit row and one `send_confirmation` task, together), the order of review reasons, the repeated-event money rule, the deadline by the event's ledger time (D-20, D-69), the lock order (the ledger row, the checkout row, the order, the payment).

`payment_record_provider_check(uuid, jsonb)` — 0006's steps (a)–(c) word for word (the validation, the locks, the money judged as the event path judges it):

4. **What the provider showed is kept for every linked attempt.** For an attempt whose provider order is `linked`, whatever its order's state — under the checkout row's and the order's locks, which serialise the check with signed events, so a webhook that committed first is seen and the observation is still recorded beside it —, each listed payment not on record gets its known row (`created`, `signature_verified = false`, the observed amount where the row can hold it — INR, within the column's bounds; otherwise the audit row is the evidence); a row that exists is left as it is; a payment id another order holds is `payment_conflict` (`ZP021`) and nothing of the call is kept. An attempt that is not linked writes nothing (as 0006).
5. **Only an order that awaits its payment, under no review other than a hold, is ever held.** `payment_status = 'pending'`, `fulfilment_status = 'pending_payment'`, unflagged or carrying a hold: exactly one payment, in INR, for both the order's total and the checkout row's amount, with no other known payment — counted as the event path counts one (contract 2): another payment row of the order that has not failed, a row without a payment id included, or, while a hold stands, a payment that another provider-check hold of the order named (*2026-11-27, `c642728`:* before it, the check counted neither, and raised a hold that the payment's own capture could not clear — the event path then made that capture a review) — and the listed payment's row not failed → 0005's hold (`capture_unverified` for a captured payment, else `authorized_only`) with one audit row `payment.hold.<hold>` (reason `provider_check`) carrying the observed money; a captured answer replaces `authorized_only`; an equal or stronger hold stands (nothing is written).
6. **Anything else is a review no later capture clears**, with one audit row `payment.review.<reason>` (reason `provider_check`; `external_ref` the strongest payment shown — the first captured, else the first authorised, by id) whose `next.payments` lists every payment as observed, in id order. The reason, in this order of precedence: `currency_mismatch` / `amount_mismatch` (another currency; an amount that is not both the order's total and the checkout row's amount); `extra_payment` (more than one live payment; one payment beside another known payment, counted as contract 5 counts it; on a confirmed order, any payment but the one whose signed capture confirmed it — the `external_ref` of its `payment.captured.confirmed` audit row); `payment_state_conflict` (a listed payment whose row says `failed`); `order_not_payable` (an order no longer awaiting its payment — fulfilment moved off `pending_payment`, or `payment_status` `expired` or `failed` —, as the event path's step (e) judges it); `order_under_review` (a payment not on record, shown for a pending order under a review that is not a hold, with no other reason).
7. **Once per distinct observation.** A review audit row is written only when the order has none with the same reason and the same observed payments (compared in id order, so a reordered listing is the same observation).
8. **A review flags, and moves nothing else.** The UPDATE sets `needs_review = true` and `payment_hold = NULL` only where the order is unflagged or carries a hold: an existing review stands as it is, and no review is ever cleared. It never changes `payment_status`, `fulfilment_status`, `confirmed_at`, item stages or pending work — a confirmed order meeting a review stays confirmed, with its items' stage and its one `send_confirmation` task.
9. **An exact repeat writes nothing** — a confirmed order's own confirmed payment at its money (a stale `authorized` listing of it included); one payment already on record, listed at the stored money with no other reason, for a pending order under a review that is not a hold; an observation already audited under the same reason.
10. **It never confirms** (D-68). Nothing in the provider check sets `payment_status = 'captured'`, moves fulfilment or an item, writes `confirmed_at` or queues work. The answer keeps 0006's shape: `held`, `payment_status`, `needs_review`, `payment_hold`.

## Security check

- **Privileges.** Neither function is `SECURITY DEFINER`; each has `set search_path = ''` and schema-qualified names. They run as `service_role` with the table privileges 0002 already granted it. The publishable key (`anon`) and a signed-in staff member (`authenticated`) cannot execute either (42501), and no table grant or policy changed: the browser still reaches no order, payment or event data.
- **No generic entry point.** No dynamic SQL, no statement assembled from an argument; `p_payments` is validated value by value exactly as under 0006, and its values are compared or stored.
- **The caller cannot name the money into a confirmation.** The provider check can raise a hold on an order that awaits its payment, or a review, and record payments as `created` and unverified — nothing more; on a confirmed order it can only flag the order and audit what it saw. `payment_process_event` still takes a ledger row id and nothing else.
- **Personal data.** None added: the audit rows carry statuses, amounts in paise, currencies, provider payment ids and ledger row ids — no name, address, email or phone.
- **Known limits.** 0007's own: (a) **A late authorisation that the check saw beside its signed failure waits for a person.** A Razorpay payment whose signed `payment.failed` was processed and which the provider check observed live — before or after that failure — ends under review (`payment_state_conflict`), so its own later signed capture is `order_under_review`: not confirmed, no `send_confirmation`, where under 0006 that capture would have confirmed. A person resolves it (the Admin's review queue is S2.2, refunds S2.4). Without a provider check in between, a late authorisation's capture confirms as before. The cost is in the safe direction (D-20, D-69). (b) **An extra payment seen after confirmation only flags the order.** The confirmed order gets `needs_review = true` and the audit row; nothing shows it to staff before S1.9 / S2.2, nothing refunds it (S2.4), and no reconciler job exists (D-68). (c) **The widened recording matters for races.** The application asks Razorpay only about an unflagged pending order — the checkout's relaunch (`src/lib/server/checkout/flow.ts`) and the order page's status question (`src/lib/server/order-confirmation.ts`) —, so a confirmed or reviewed order meets the check when a signed event committed during the provider's read (finding 2's race; R3-1); an order whose fulfilment no longer awaits its payment (none at S1.7 — S2.11) is asked about directly, since neither caller reads the fulfilment status first, and gets `order_not_payable`. Carried from the 0006 record, as it states them there: its (c) — a `created` (in-flight) payment beside a live one is not counted as live; its (d) — a failed event's money is judged on neither path, by design; its (f) — handed to S2.11: the first launch right after a link does not check the fulfilment status, and a cancelled order's page shows the closed state's wording. Superseded: the 0006 record's (a), by `86bb63c`'s checkout change; closed: its (b) and (e), by this file.
